I am a Lecturer in Criminology in the School of Criminology, Sociology and Policing at the [University of Hull](https://www.hull.ac.uk/staff-directory/daniel-marciniak), which I joined in 2022.

My research looks at how digital technologies, data analysis and artificial intelligence are changing the practice and governance of policing. I use ethnographic and interview-based methods to study what happens when tools such as predictive policing systems and automated risk scores are put to work in police organisations, and how officers, analysts and technology companies negotiate them in practice.

Before joining Hull, I was a member of the research group [Anthropology of AI in Policing and Justice](https://www.eth.mpg.de/anthropologyAI) at the Max Planck Institute for Social Anthropology. I earned my doctorate in sociology at the University of Essex, within the Human Rights, Big Data and Technology project, with a thesis on the reshaping and fragility of socio-technical relations in a digital policing environment.

Research interests
------
* Technology in policing
* Artificial intelligence, machine learning and data analysis in policing
* Governmentality and quantification
* Surveillance studies
* Science and technology studies
