PhD thesis in sociology, completed within the Human Rights, Big Data and Technology project at the University of Essex.

The thesis shows the multifaceted character of data-driven technology in policing and the socio-technical negotiations that take place in operational settings. Against techno-deterministic accounts of predictive and digital policing, it finds complex co-constructions of risk, suspicion and priorities that depend on operational context, while also showing how technologies have affordances of their own: risk scores, for example, institutionalise a focus on repeat offenders.
